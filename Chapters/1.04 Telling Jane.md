After she got home from the appointment with the blood test, Sara was laying in bed on her phone. And found herself down a rabbit hole online, specifically a thread where people were complaining about morning sickness. Right, because she'd never ran until she puked, no... she rolled her eyes. Was it healthy? Probably not. Had she pushed her body to that extreme before? Multiple times. 

She had to agree that the various common symptoms of pregnancy sounded really uncomfortable; downright painful and unpleasant. But physical discomfort, including pain, was no stranger to her. It was part of getting her tattoos and now part of becoming an athlete. The price of entry you could say. She could handle pain. She was willing to deal with the pain and the risks and all of it for her goal of having a child of her own. 

At the same time... everything she saw online agreed with what her doctor had said: unless she wanted to risk the pregnancy, her workouts were going to need to change; everything high intensity had to go. And not here soon, starting yesterday. Not that she would ever trust the internet over her doctor, but death by a thousand cuts (or sources in this case) helped to counter the urge she felt to use her punch bag to let off steam. Sara put her phone down, and sighed. That urge to kick and punch was a bit less, but it wasn't gone. Understanding helped some, but it didn't solve it or really do much. 

Giving up the intensity that kept her steady was going to be a seriously difficult adjustment. But, at the same time, risking losing this pregnancy? She couldn't live with that, not when this might be literally her only chance at having her own biological child, her own family. She hadn't let herself hope, and now... the positive blood test result was folded up in her wallet, and the doctor had said based on it, she was maybe at the 2 month mark, give or take. 

And yet, it didn't feel... real. Her body didn't feel that different, but the doctor had said that was normal too. Actually he'd pointed out that in very rare cases there were people didn't know until birth, that every case was utterly unique, and they would know exactly where she was at her ultrasound he'd scheduled for a month from now. 

Sara heard the front door, then someone moving about the house. Meaning Jane was home from work. Sara put away her phone, moved the edge of the bed, and then stood. If she was doing this, and she absolutely was doing this, then she needed to tell her wife about it. She had no idea what she was going to say, but she didn't want to wait. It hadn't even been a full week of not pushing to her usual level and barely around a day of not following her usual exercise schedule, but she already felt antsy. She needed to tell Jane what was going on before the feeling got worse. How Jane was going to react... as Sara left the bedroom she realized she had no idea what Jane would think of her anger. It had pushed so many people away already. 

Jane was in the living room, admiring another new painting Sara hadn't seen before that was now hanging on their wall. Jane must have painted it while she was at work. When she heard Sara, Jane turned. "Hey dear," she smiled, "how did the appointment go? They get it figured out?" 

(I'm picturing Jane is over by the TV and Sara is near the kitchen, so they're pretty much in opposite corners of the living room from each other; Sara stops and creates the distance)

Last night, Sara had told Jane she was going seeing the doctor today, but the only reason she'd given Jane was that her joints didn't feel right. "Yeah, I'm..." She swallowed and tried to gather her words. She found herself putting her hands in her pockets. "I'm going to have to make some pretty big changes, for a while. Because... I'm pregnant." 

Jane just sort of stopped for a moment, then, "You're... pregnant?"

She wished she could have have taken a long hard run to clear her head before trying to have this conversation. Not that is was an option. "Yeah. They took a blood test, it was positive." 

Jane's eyes went big, then her hands went to her mouth, "You're pregnant? Sorry, I just asked that. Sorry." She paused. "This just doesn't feel real."

"Tell me about it." Sara smiled a little at Jane's cute excitement. She loved that about her. 

"Do you know how far along you are?" Jane glance down to Sara's middle for a moment. Nothing to see there, not yet anyway.  

"He said maybe two months, but can't be sure at this point." Sara shrugged her shoulders. 

"So did the doctor..." Jane paused, "did the doctor say if it's a boy or a girl?"

"It's too early for that." Sara shifted her weight. "But there's something else... Something that you need to know. Mind if we sit?" 

Jane frowned a little. "I don't mind, do you want to sit on the couch?" She held out her hand towards it. 

"Sure." Sara took her hands out of her pockets, and they both both sat on the couch. 

"You know I do a lot of really high intensity workouts. Those aren't just because I want to be an athlete. Actually that's not why I started doing them at all." Sara wasn't sure she'd ever said this out loud to anyone else before. "I get really intense anger sometimes, well, regularly, and that's how I've been dealing with it since around when I started high school." She held one hand with the other. "The thing is, I can't keep doing those really intense workouts while I'm pregnant, it would put me at risk of losing... it." That felt so weird to say aloud. Sara paused and took a breath to brace herself. "So I want to go ahead and apologize before I get snippy with you, I don't want to, but-" This was freaking her out more than a match, but she tried to focus. And not sound like she was making excuses. "Look, I'm going to try to find other ways to manage that anger," Sara swallowed, "but it might take me a while to adjust. So like I said, I want to go ahead and say I'm sorry now." 

Jane looked down at her own hands, and the bits of paint on them. "My art is how I deal with my emotions. If I injured my hands and couldn't paint for months, I would be a mess." Jane's fingers curled and she reflexively brought them a bit closer to herself without even meaning to. And Sara was choosing something like that so they could start a family? Jane relaxed her hands, and offered then to Sara. "I would have to find another way to express myself until my hands healed, otherwise I would lose my mind." That was the nice name for the dark place Jane didn't like think about. 

Sara looked between Jane's face and her hands. She understood? Sara let her hands slide into Jane's. 

"Do you want help finding other ways to manage it?" Jane squeezed her hands. 

Sara nodded. "Yeah, that would be really nice." Sara felt just a little of the tension leave her, she leaned closer to Jane, and put her head on Jane's shoulder. 

Then Jane put her arms around Sara, and rubbed on her back a little. 

Sara's breath shifted automatically and she felt a few of her muscles relax. "Thank you." She whispered as she put her arms around Jane as well.

## Author's notes
The character Sara's pregnancy is actually at 1 month at this point in the plot, but she doesn't find that out until the ultrasound. 

The first part of this scene (where she's alone on the bed) show the character Sara thinking about temporarily giving up her intense workout vs giving up on her pregnancy, and it's no contest for her: this character wants her own biological children more than anything. She willing gives up her intense workouts temporarily (during the pregnancy) knowing it will be difficult for her but more than worth it to her. This character also knows not doing these workouts will be temporary, as after she gives birth, she will not be pregnant and therefore able to do high intensity workouts without risking the things or people (other characters) that she loves. She knows it is not permanent and she will be able to do her workout again later. 

The first part of this scene shows that the character Sara doesn't have much of a reaction to the pain and discomfort of pregnancy. Because she is used to her goals requiring pain and discomfort in her body. This is nothing new to her, and for her is a small price to pay for entry into having a family of her own. At this point in the story, the character Sara wants a family, she wants her own biological children more than anything. 

The first part of this scene mainly shows that Sara is choosing to keep the pregnancy and carry it to term on her own, before even her wife knows about it. She makes this choice fully willingly and she is fully informed about how difficult it will be for her and what she will need to temporarily give up to make that happen. This is very healthy, because she is the only one pregnant, therefore it is her decision to make and one she should make on her own. 

In the second part of this scene, the character Sara is approaching the character Jane with her decision about her own body already made already made, and Jane respects Sara's decision. Sara is scared the anger will push Jane away, because Sara's anger has pushed away other characters before, but again Sara has already decided carrying her pregnancy to term is what she wants to do because to her that is worth all of this, because that's how much she values having her own biological children. This further shows just how deeply important this is to Sara. It also shows how much Sara wants family and connection, even if vulnerability and her own emotions are things she struggles with. 

Sara admitting about her anger to Jane is hard because Sara is used to managing her internal world alone. No one else in her past has been able to weather the storm within her. Sara thinks she believes Jane, but there will be some subconscious part of her that will be scared Jane is going to change her mind anyway. So in later scenes when Jane shows up repeatedly, this plus Sara's deep desire for family & connection are why Sara feels so strongly in the later scene with the ice pack.
