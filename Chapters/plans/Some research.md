*   **The Partner’s Role:** Her nurturing partner doesn't need to "fix" her anger. They just need to **validate the difficulty**. *"It makes sense that you feel restless. You’re an athlete. Let’s figure out what 'active recovery' looks like for your mind right now."*

### 4. Narrative Arc: From "Burning" to "Flowing"
*   **Old Mindset:** Anger = Fuel for the fire (Workout).
*   **New Mindset:** Anger = Signal to pause and breathe (Regulation).
*   **The Conflict:** It feels **inefficient** and **frustrating** to her at first. She wants the *efficiency* of a hard workout to clear her head. The new methods feel slow and subtle.
*   **The Growth:** She learns that **vulnerability** (admitting she’s struggling to regulate) actually deepens the bond with her partner more than her previous "silent strength" did. She realizes that **managing** the anger in real-time, rather than deferring it, makes her more present in her relationship.

This approach keeps her competent and strong while realistically portraying the physiological constraints of a twin pregnancy.
