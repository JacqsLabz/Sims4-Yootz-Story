
"I'm sick of being the only normal human!" Savanna yelled. Her tone went rude, mocking her sisters, *"What does blood taste like? Ug, needing to use doors is so annoying."*

Sara clinch a fist to ground herself, "What does it matter if your sisters are different from you?" She didn't understand how that connected to this, so she tried to listen. 

"What does it matter?" Savanna repeated as if Sara was out of her mind. "What does it matter if I'm surrounded by monsters?"

Sara opened her mouth to speak, but Savanna cut her off before she even made a sound. 

"I saw you on the camping trip, so don't even. All vampires are like that, aren't they? Monsters with fangs, glowing eyes, and messing with people's minds to drink their blood. It's not only you, is it?" Savanna said to Sara, then turned to Jane, "How often do you go out and hunt people? What about Lilith and Don? Does Anne? Is that why she was asking about it, because you were teaching her how to prey on people?" 

Jane gasped and put a hand over her mouth. She didn't know how Savanna could say things like calling them monsters. She blinked away tears; didn't their own daughter know what they were like? 

Sara decided to ignore the obviously mean things said in anger, and instead went for what was directly untrue. "No, Anne doesn't have fangs. She is... different from everyone else." Somehow she managed to keep her voice calm despite the feelings in her. She would deal with those later. 

Savanna's voice went dark, "Everyone in this house is different. I don't know what you think that thing you call Emma is, but she is not your daughter." 

"Do not talk about your sister that way." Jane said, brittle and full of ice. It was one thing to call a pair of vampires monsters, but calling Emma a thing? Jane wouldn't have it. 

Sara didn't blame Jane, those words were out of line, but Savanna obviously angry, "Look, we're all upset here. Let's take some time to cool down and-" 

"No, I'm done with this house! I'm leaving!" Savanna yelled over what Sara was trying to say. 

"And going where exactly?" Sara snapped back. "Got somewhere else you're going to sleep tonight?"

"Are you going to stop me!? Take control of my mind!?" Savanna screamed.

Jane once again found herself so hurt she was at a loss for words. She couldn't even do that, and just because Sara could do something didn't mean she would. Even back when they were both human, Sara had been physically strong enough to over power Jane when they moved in together, and had always, always made sure to check that strength. Even the few times when her anger hadn't been in check. 

"No." Sara said simply, leaning forward on the couch to rub her face so she didn't snap at her daughter again. Part of her wanted to hope she hadn't been this bad when she was Savanna's age, but Sara knew better: she'd been worse. At least some of that was because Savanna had her real family. 

"Then I'm out of here!" Savanna stormed off to her room to pack a bag. 

"But..." Jane didn't even know how to put it into words. 

"We all need to calm down. This will give her some time to cool off, to clear her head." Sara flexed her fists. "When she does, she'll be back." To her home, her family, where she belonged. That had been a lot of the issue for Sara: she hadn't had that growing up. 

Sara traced the lines of one of her tattoos on one arm with the other hand she made her way to her workout room. No body said raising teens was going to be easy, but she hadn't been prepared for this. Sara changed, wrapped her hands, toned down her speed, held back every bit of her strength, and then punched and kicked as hard as she could like that. In a way, she supposed it shouldn't have surprised her that her daughter was a hot head too. Of course at least one of them would take after her like that. But, at the same time, Sara thought so much of her anger came from things like never having a real home before, from not belonging anywhere. Building that with Jane had certainly helped her a lot. She had thought, she'd hoped, that having a home and a family would have helped them not get this angry. It had Sara questioning her own anger far more as she went through her routine. How much did she actually understand about herself and what she felt?

As far as what Savanna said, Sara knew from experience the kid wouldn't mean half of it when she calmed down and got to where she could be level headed about things again. For Sara it had often been so much more than half, a few times all of it. 

In the meantime, Sara also knew actions were more important then words. So she took a few minutes on her phone to first top off the kid debit card they'd given Savanna, and looked over the kid restrictions. Sara made she to up her daily spending limit, she was probably going to need it. She also setup a savings account with an automatic deposit. That way she could show Savanna that their promise of support wasn't hollow words.
