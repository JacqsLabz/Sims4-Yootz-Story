I guess I'm trying to get Grant to this place where he's still got trauma issues, and it's hard (even with his disoriented mental framework), but he starts to think about what he truly wants. Having that being something in his mind I think would help give him forward momentum. A better future to build towards. Because I don't want this to be able him just sort of stopping. Like yes sure he's resting, but I want to weave in more of that natural curiosity to his healing process. Because at first he almost doesn't respond to the supernatural everything because he's too focused on Ben. He gets just the smallest piece of resting for a moment (Jane's purple eyes, Ben taking to her, and Jane making dinner), which causes him to dose off in the chair. 

Then a small piece of safety (the popcorn), and that gets him to a point where things line up funny: he's relaxed enough for his true personality to come out (using his curiosity for something other than taking care of Ben) before this safety causes the trauma processing to really start up in a serious fashion. Everything he's been doing to hold himself together without support is still locked down, but he's safe for the moment so he gets a small window where he can just enjoy a dinner in the tiny cabin and his natural innate curiosity shows when Emma turns into her ghost form. But that doesn't last, because the safety means all the locked down crap starts opening and coming out. It starts with him sleeping all night & most of the day. That's his brain processing a lot stuff. But as we see when he wakes up, he's still on a hairpin trigger. This is the lockdown of everything he hasn't dealt with trying to reassert itself, because things are leaking out so some part of him is seeking familiar danger. We can tell he's not in his typical old full lockdown, because he's more tired and it's harder to be attentive to Ben. But then this new safe reality wins out: Emma handle everything while he was sleeping. His body literally does not know how to handle it: his body got all geared up and ready for another hard day of handling everything because no one else would, but there's no "race to run" so there's no where for all that energy to go. He's never felt that before, so it's unfamiliar ("wrong"). Thankfully Emma offers him some soothing tea, and if nothing else it helps with the mild dehydration from not drinking anything all day. 

Plus like a typical trauma survivor, Grant is to some degree numb to / discounted from his body and therefore it's needs. As he's moving to safety, this affect is going to fade (healing involves getting more in touch with one's body), because there's not a more important emergency to handle. So from Grant's POV, this is going to feel like his body is being really whiny (about things like hunger). Because he's used to having to keep going even when his body is asking for him to stop & while he has no emotional support, creating a sort of protective numbness (hence why he didn't feel hungry when Jane first asked what he wanted to eat, but once he ate some popcorn and smelled the sausage and such, realized he was & had plenty to eat). Listening to his body just wasn't an option for a long time. 

On the way to the Falls, they visit Anne. With her fangs, glowing red eyes, superhuman daughters, casual "$100k or so", and proper elite hostess nature. Grant won't be scared of Anne, despite her physical appearance, because he reads body language more than physical appearance. He doesn't know it, but he's basically always scanning for Savanna's next hot-headed rage episode. His focus is on things like how Anne hugs Emma, offers them tea, and how she moves around. And as finely tuned hostess, Anne will of course make any tiny adjustment she can to make sure her guest is comfortable. To some degree. She is just around family, so to some degree she's more herself and less the prefect hostess as she's not in the spotlight. 

So they go to the Falls. And Grant can't sleep. His body looking for the emergency... when there is none, which just causes it to look harder (if you spot a snake then don't see it, you don't assume it's gone, you assume it's hiding; same concept). All the undealt with stuff starts coming out in force as him and Emma are at the Falls. Because he's so safe. It's like a paradox: the better he does, the worse he feels. Sure having some soothing tea after a night of nightmares and lack of sleep is good. But it signals safety, which causes hit to hit another "trauma landmine" so to speak. The more he has signal for safety, the more stuff that's going to come out and demand processing. Which can create it's own form of overwhelm, and I'd rather Grant not get stuck in that loop. Because it's essentially getting stuck in freaking out because there isn't an emergency. That loops is a lot harder to get out of, so I'd rather not go there with him. 

Anyway, while they're at the cabin Grant realizes this is basically an adjustment period. It's temporary. Which will help him deal with it. Oh and another thing that will help him handle will be if he realizes this is basically a tax for taking care of Ben the way he did. Because he won't mind that at all, much like how Sara doesn't mind her withered stomach. I mean his isn't as permanent. But his system is majorly fried, and healing from that is a high price to pay, but Grant wouldn't change a damn thing. Other than his mother refusing the help of the family. Given that she did that, he wouldn't change anything about what he did for Ben. He doesn't mind paying this high price. But I want it to be more than that. This isn't more of the same, this isn't more of him being there for Ben. Like I was saying earlier, having him realize that his life can be anything he wants it to be. Because then he can realize that he has a future after he gets through the worst of this healing. And that future can be anything he wants it to be. Sure he's paying the high prices of healing from what he did for Ben, and he doesn't mind that, but I don't want him to be healing for Ben. Because it won't work. He has to be doing the healing for himself, or he can't heal. that's just how trauma works. He has to learn to stop the whole keep going when his body says it needs food water rest and how to navigate having other there to support him. He has to learn to put himself first. He has to learn to care about not just his needs, but his wants. He has to allow himself to have hopes, wants, and dreams. He can recognize that the extremes he went to for Ben as harmful to himself, and not regret doing that, but the way out is decide to live for himself. 

Which I mean of course Ben will be part of his ideal intentional life, but Ben won't be every part of it. And I think the way he gets there is by realizing he can live for himself. Not that he won't do things for others, but that he'll take care of himself first instead of always pouring from a glass that has been empty for years. And I think this imagining a future defined by what he wants is going to require his natural curiosity. I think the two go hand in hand, he has ask "What do I want?" because he can answer "I want a better life" But I don't know how to get him there. I don't know what his curiosity coming back looks like. Like sure it came back for the moment, but I'm looking at the big long term permeant rising that's going to grow and last for the rest of his life. I mean sure maybe it's linear, but again I'm getting nothing for what this looks like.
