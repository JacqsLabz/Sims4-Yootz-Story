Sara is not a typical "pregnant athlete" she doesn't think of herself that way. She doesn't hold "athlete" has part of her identity. Her is using working in the athletic career as a way to make money using something she does anyway: intense workouts. This also gives her more time for more exercise which helps keep her moods/emotions/feelings more stable. It is more of a means to an ends to her. She needs incomes and she wants to spent time working out, so early in her life (before her second pregnancy) is doing both at once. 

Sara's pain tolerance doesn't come from her being an athlete. She got most of her tattoos before she got into sports in high school. Sara has consider (especially physical and/or bodily) pain and discomfort to be a small price to pay to achieve her goals and get her desires her whole life. She didn't enjoy the needle stabbing her over and over to get her extensive and very large multiple tattoos but she valued having them more, so she consented to the pain. She does this through her whole life and the whole story: Sara often consents to pain and discomfort because she values something else more. 

Sara does view her body as a tool, one she takes good care of. This view allows her to maintain her agency in situations where other would feel like their body is out of their control. Because she's not trying to control every detail of her body, she knows that isn't physically possible, and so she focus on the big picture. Including but not limited to during her intense workouts and her pregnancies. When her hands are in pain the day after a heavy punch bag routine, she doesn't feel like her hands are out of her control or like her body is failing her. Sara views that pain and discomfort as in her control because she choose how much, how hard, and how long she punched for. To her that pain and discomfort is part of her choice that she made willingly, again because to places a higher value on doing the punching. 

At her core, Sara is extremely loving. She often expresses her love through her fierce protectiveness. Before she had a family, she wanted a family that she could love by protecting more than anything, so much that it hurt to think about. So she didn't. She focused on her workouts, her job, and Jane. And considered herself lucky to at least have someone that loved her for who she is. 

When Sara throws away the first pregnancy test this is not her acting in denial. Between fertility issues and irregular cycles on top of that, her chances of actually getting pregnant are super lower.  Sara doesn't believe the home pregnancy test because she knows they can be unreliable. A home urine pregnancy test is not equal to a clinic blood pregnancy test. A home urine test CAN be a false positive, but a clinic's blood test won't be a false positive. Detecting the hormone in the urine enough to make the test read positive does not always mean pregnancy is happening. A doctor measuring it in the blood doesn't get a simple yes/no, they get an amount of the hormone. It's much more complex and precise. Also measuring blood is not the same as measuring urine. Sara assumes the home test is a false positive because she knows that can happen and according to what she knows that is the next most likely thing. The thing with the high percent chance of happening is that the test is defective and the hormone isn't in her urine. Next is the test is a false positive meaning there is a reason other than pregnancy for the hormone to be in her urine. Because she sees her chances of being pregnant as that low, to her it's a percentage that's a number that's lower than the chance of a defective test and lower than the chance of a false positive. Sara was told she would probably never get pregnant, and then she started her training for the athlete career, which made her cycles irregular, which made her chances of getting pregnant even lower. This isn't denial, this is blunt honesty about her odds of getting what she wants. She's not in denial, she's avoiding being unrealistic. She's settled for maintaining her sanity. The Jane entered her life, so she appreciates having a partner to protect, and considers herself lucky to even have Jane in her life.
